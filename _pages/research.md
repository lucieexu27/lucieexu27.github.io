## Job Market Paper: 

**"Privacy Regulation and Optimal Product Design"**

_Abstract:_ This paper studies how privacy regulation shapes a seller’s product design. In a circular city environment, the firm chooses a product design before observing a noisy signal of a consumer’s preferences and setting a personalized price. Privacy regulation determines the informativeness of this signal and limits the firm’s ability to target consumers. I show that the seller’s optimal product design responds non-monotonically to privacy. Greater privacy can induce either broader or more niche products. The extent depends on how it interacts with the firm’s pricing incentives. I then examine the resulting implications for consumer welfare.

## Works in Progress

**"Optimal Consumer Privacy with an Expert"**

_Abstract:_ I study consumer privacy choice in a setting where a consumer, who is uncertain about which product suits him, receives a product recommendation from an expert. The expert has some intrinsic concern for match suitability (honest recommendation) but can be influenced by commissions offered by the product providers. I show that more precise information about consumers leads the recommended firm to charge a higher price, while less precise information leads firms to compete in commissions, which extracts the consumer's surplus entirely. The consumer-optimal amount of information is therefore the least precise that still deters commissions. I then show that when commissions are disclosed to the consumer, an equilibrium without commissions always exists, and commissions are ruled out entirely for a wider range of the expert's concern for suitability. Disclosure therefore never lowers the consumer's surplus and allows him to share less information.
