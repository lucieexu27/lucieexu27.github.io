## Job Market Paper: 

**"Privacy Regulation and Optimal Product Design"**

_Abstract:_ This paper studies how privacy regulation shapes a seller’s product design. In a circular city environment, the firm chooses a product design before observing a noisy signal of a consumer’s preferences and setting a personalized price. Privacy regulation determines the informativeness of this signal and limits the firm’s ability to target consumers. I show that the seller’s optimal product design responds non-monotonically to privacy. Greater privacy can induce either broader or more niche products. The extent depends on how it interacts with the firm’s pricing incentives. I then examine the resulting implications for consumer welfare.

## Works in Progress

**"Optimal Consumer Privacy with an Expert"**

_Abstract:_ I study consumer privacy choice in a setting where a consumer, who is uncertain about which product suits him, receives a product recommendation from an expert. The expert has some intrinsic concern for match suitability (honest recommendation) but can be influenced by commissions offered by the product providers. I show that more precise information about consumers leads the recommended firm to charge a higher price, while less precise information leads firms to compete in commissions, which extracts the consumer's surplus entirely. The consumer-optimal amount of information is therefore the least precise that still deters commissions. I then show that disclosing commissions to the consumer eliminates them in equilibrium. Disclosure thus protects the consumer's surplus regardless of the expert's concern for suitability, and frees him to share as little information as he likes
